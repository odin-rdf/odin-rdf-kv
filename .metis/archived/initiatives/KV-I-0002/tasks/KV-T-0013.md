# Page-reuse verification, env_stats and cost measurement

## Parent Initiative

[[KV-I-0002]]

## Objective

Verify the initiative end to end, and close out its exit criteria:
- held readers in the randomized model;
- the steady-state and long-reader criteria;
- threads under TSan;
- the flat free list's commit cost, which decides whether the extent fallback is needed;
- `env_stats` (REQ-008), and the Linux runs.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] **`env_stats(env) -> Stats`** with `last_pgno`, `file_pages`, `free_ready`, `free_pending`, `readers` and `oldest_reader` (0 if none). It's documented as extensible by step 6, and is safe to call from any thread; it takes each lock briefly.
- [x] **Model test:** `run_model` holds up to 4 read transactions, each opened at a random point and released after a random number of commits. On release, each reader's full forward scan and random `get`s must match the oracle state it began at. `space_check` runs after every commit and reopen. Run it for at least 10⁵ operations with reopens.
- [x] **Steady state (NFR-004):** 1,000 keys with mixed inline and overflow values are overwritten over 10⁴ commits with no readers, and `last_pgno` stops growing after the first few hundred commits. Record the bound it reaches.
- [x] **Long reader:** with one reader held, the file grows for a while. After it ends, a further 10³ commits add no pages, and `free_ready` falls as they are reused.
- [x] **Threads:** extend `test_snapshot_isolation_across_threads` so that readers start and end continually while the writer overwrites, forcing reuse. Every reader checksum matches its snapshot. It passes under `-sanitize:thread` on macOS arm64.
- [x] **Cost measurement:** time commits of a one-key update against free-list sizes of 10², 10³, 10⁴ and 10⁵ pages, including the run search. Record the numbers and a verdict on the extent fallback in the Status Updates and in KV-I-0002's Results. If the fallback is warranted, file it as a backlog item rather than doing it here.
- [x] **Build matrix:** `scripts/test.sh` on macOS (debug, speed, ASan, and the cross-`odin check` targets), and `scripts/test-linux.sh arm64` and `amd64`.
- [x] **Docs:**
  - KV-I-0002's exit-criteria table (REQ-001 to REQ-008, NFR-001 to NFR-004) with evidence, plus Results, deviations and known limitations;
  - the vision's Current State;
  - `CLAUDE.md`'s "Next up" and code map (`freelist.odin`, `space_check`).

## Test Cases

### Test Case 1: Model with held readers
- **Test ID:** TC-001
- **Preconditions:** a fresh database with `map_size` of 1 GiB.
- **Steps:**
  1. Run 10⁵ random operations with random commit and abort boundaries, holding readers across commits, and reopen every 10⁴ operations (after releasing the readers).
  2. Check each reader against its snapshot's oracle on release.
  3. Run `space_check` after each commit and reopen.
- **Expected results:** no mismatches, and no accounting failures. The seed is printed on failure.

### Test Case 2: Steady-state plateau
- **Test ID:** TC-002
- **Preconditions:** 1,000 keys committed.
- **Steps:** make 10⁴ commits, each overwriting 1–20 random keys, and sample `last_pgno` every 100 commits.
- **Expected results:** the last 50 samples are equal.

### Test Case 3: Map-sized steady state
- **Test ID:** TC-003
- **Preconditions:** a `map_size` just large enough for the data plus a margin.
- **Steps:** make 10⁴ overwrite commits.
- **Expected results:** no `Map_Full`.

## Implementation Notes

### Technical Approach
- The model's held readers are `kv.Txn` values in a fixed array, plus a copy of the oracle at their start. The oracles are copied with the temporary allocator and freed on release.
- The cost measurement lives as a test guarded by `when #config(KV_BENCH, false)`, or in a small `bench/` program, so the normal suite doesn't depend on timing. Choose one and record why.

### Dependencies
KV-T-0010, KV-T-0011 and KV-T-0012.

### Risk Considerations
- The plateau bound depends on D1's one-commit delay and on split behaviour. Assert that it plateaus, not an exact figure.
- Linux tests need OrbStack: `orb start` first and `orb stop` after.

## Status Updates

### 2026-09-24: implemented

**What was built**
- **`env_stats(env) -> Stats`** (`kv/env.odin`), with `last_pgno`, `file_pages`, `free_ready`, `free_pending`, `readers` (live read transactions, not slots) and `oldest_reader` (0 if none).
  - The doc comment says step 6 extends it.
  - It takes only `snapshot_mutex`, never `writer_mutex` (see Decisions). The writer's figures live in `Env.stats`, which is updated under that lock:
    - at open;
    - after a release at `txn_begin(rw)` that moved anything;
    - when `file_grow` resizes the file;
    - and when a commit publishes its snapshot.
  - `txn_commit` now swaps `Env.free` just before publishing the snapshot rather than just after; both still come after the meta sync.
- **Held readers in the model** (`tests/model_test.odin`). `run_model` holds up to `max_held` (default 4) read transactions:
  - Each begins at a random operation (1 in 500), with a copy of the committed model, and is released after 1–20 further commits, or before a reopen or a `Map_Full` reopen.
  - On release it must pass `model_diff` (`get` of every id, full forward and backward scans, `tree_check`) and `space_check`.
  - `Model_Stats` gained `held_readers`, `held_commits` and `reused_pages` (dirty pages at or below the begin snapshot's `last_pgno`), and `test_model_randomized` asserts minimums for them.
  - A typical run: 115 readers held across 999 commits in total, 37,897 pages reused, 9 reopens.
- **`tests/steady_test.odin`** (new), 4 tests. The shared workload `steady_commit` overwrites 1–20 of 1,000 keys per commit, 15% of values in overflow runs of 1–4 pages, and runs `space_check` and `tree_check` after every commit.
  - `test_env_stats`:
    - the figures against the env's own state on a fresh file;
    - after commits, with three readers on two snapshots;
    - after the readers end (nothing is released until a write transaction begins);
    - from the thread holding a write transaction: the release shows at once, what the transaction takes only after its commit;
    - after a reopen.
  - `test_steady_state_plateau` (TC-002): 10⁴ commits with `last_pgno` sampled every 100. The assertion was restated (see Decisions).
  - `test_steady_state_full_map` (TC-003):
    - Values keep their size, so the data has one. The map is the data plus a margin argued from the workload: twice one commit's worst case (a leaf and a 4-page run per key, the branch pages, the free-list run), plus `put`'s reserve.
    - A held reader first fills the map (a commit is refused with `Map_Full`). Then 10⁴ commits run on reused pages alone.
  - `test_steady_state_long_reader`:
    - 200 commits, then a reader held for 300. While it is held, each commit adds pending pages, and `env_stats` reports the reader.
    - The reader still reads its snapshot, and passes `space_check`.
    - After it ends, 1,000 more commits: no put extends the file, and everything else is as in Decisions.
- **Threads** (`tests/isolation_test.odin`). After the original phase (four readers frozen on one snapshot for 100 commits), a churn phase:
  - Four threads keep beginning read transactions, scanning each one 1–3 times, and ending it, while the writer makes 200 commits of 50 overwrites (5% overflow values).
  - Each scan checks that the keys ascend and the entry count matches, and compares a sum of per-entry hashes with the checksum the writer recorded for that snapshot. The writer updates the checksum incrementally and records it before publishing.
  - Readers also call `env_stats` and check that they are counted.
  - The writer waits for a read transaction to finish before each commit, so the two interleave however fast commits are.
  - It asserts at least 200 read transactions and more than 2,000 reused pages. Typical: about 830 read transactions, about 14,000 pages reused.
- **Cost measurement** (`tests/bench_test.odin`, KV_BENCH): hand-written lists of about 0, 10², 10³, 10⁴ and 10⁵ reusable pages, all pending at open, laid out in one run or every other page. It times:
  - the begin that releases the whole list;
  - medians of 40 one-key updates (begin, put, commit);
  - a 2-page `page_alloc` in aborted transactions.

  To use it, `Hand_List`, `open_hand_list` and `HAND_TXN` in `freelist_test.odin` are no longer file-private.
- **`space_check` after every commit** now also in `commit_entries` (commit_test), `commit_one` (reader_test, on the write transaction before the commit), `alloc_test`'s setup, and both writer loops of the isolation test. `expect_latest_ok` moved from `reuse_test.odin` (file-private) to `steady_test.odin` (shared).

**Measurements**

*Steady state* (NFR-004), for the plateau workload: the data loads at 352 pages. Over 40 seeds, run for 2 × 10⁴ commits in a scratch copy with `os_sync` stubbed:
- `last_pgno` reaches 400–496 after 500 commits, 459–523 after 10⁴, and about 500 after 10⁵ (4 seeds).
- The last growth came as late as commit 19,907.
- 20 of 40 seeds grew between commits 5,000 and 10,000, by 2–57 pages.
- Without reuse (deliberate check 2) the file reaches 138,935 pages in 10⁴ commits.
- Inline-only values (35 pages of data): 72–77 pages from 10³ to 10⁵ commits, still with growth as late as commit 95,473.
- The live tree itself swings between 286 and 395 pages as values move in and out of overflow runs.

*Growth steps:* logged per put, they were overflow puts that found no contiguous run while 4–26 reusable pages were untaken, or rare peaks in what D1 keeps in flight. A scratch experiment where single pages prefer isolated reusable pages changed the plateau by at most 6 pages, so breaking runs with single pages is not the main cause.

*Full map:* data 352 pages, margin 217, map 576 pages. In experiments on the same workload, even a 64-page margin had no `Map_Full` over 10⁴ commits after a fill (8 seeds). Without the fill, one seed in six hit one `Map_Full`.

*Long reader:* while held for 300 commits, the file grows from about 420 to 7,600–8,400 pages. In the next 1,000 commits:
- puts added 0 pages in all 16 seeds;
- the free list's run placement extended the file 0–3 times (12 seeds none, 2 × 29 pages, 1 × 60, 1 × 90; run length 29–32);
- about 11,000 pages were reused;
- `free_ready` stayed level at about 7,100–7,700.

*Cost of the flat list:* macOS arm64, `-o:speed`, medians over 40 one-key updates. The "stubbed" column is the same measurement in a scratch copy with `os_sync` stubbed, since two `F_FULLFSYNC`s (about 8 ms) hide everything else.

**Verdict on the extent fallback: not needed for cost at the sizes this store targets, not filed.**
- Rewriting the list costs about 4.5 ns per record. On macOS that is under 1% of a synced commit up to 10⁴ records, and about 5% at 10⁵ (400 MB free).
- On Linux with a 1 ms `fsync` it is about 5% at 10⁴ records, but at 10⁵ it nearly doubles a small commit. That size takes a long-lived reader on a large database, since free pages are never returned. If it matters, the extent fallback, or anything that shrinks the list, is the lever.
- KV-T-0012's open concerns, measured:
  - the merge at `txn_begin(rw)`: 54–76 µs per begin at 10⁵ records, and it allocates only when `ready` outgrows its capacity;
  - the linear run search: 70–79 µs per failed search at 10⁵ scattered records;
  - the conservative check at a full map: 10⁴ commits with no `Map_Full`.
- What does matter is fragmentation: run placement and run searches. Two backlog candidates are described in KV-I-0002's known limitations.

**Decisions and deviations**
- **`env_stats` never takes `writer_mutex`.** That mutex is held for a write transaction's whole life, so taking it would make `env_stats` wait for the writer, and deadlock on the writer's own thread. The figures are therefore the last commit's plus any release since. This covers "takes each lock briefly" with one lock.
- **TC-002's "the last 50 samples are equal" is not what the design delivers under a random workload.** It failed for about half the seeds (above).
  - The test now asserts that the second half of the commits adds at most 1% of the pages it writes (observed at most 0.1%; no reuse gives over 100%), and that the file stays within twice its loaded size.
  - It logs the bound it reached and the commit of the last growth.
- **Long reader:**
  - The criterion "a further 10³ commits add no pages" holds for puts, which the test asserts exactly. The commit's free-list run may still extend the file, as described above; the test allows up to five runs' worth and logs the actual growth.
  - "`free_ready` falls as they are reused" does not happen: each commit frees about as many pages as it takes, so `free_ready` stays level, and it is logged. What the test asserts instead is that more than 5,000 pages are reused and no put grows the file.
- **TC-003 uses fixed value sizes and a held-reader fill,** so "just large enough for the data" has a meaning and every allocation after the fill is a reuse.
- **The held-reader model:**
  - Oracle copies use one preallocated slot per reader (temp allocator) rather than a copy freed on release.
  - `test_model_until_map_full` passes `max_held = 0`: a held reader fills its 5 MiB map within 12–30 commits, before much is reused, which is not what that test is for.
- **The cost measurement is a guarded test, not a `bench/` program.** It reuses the test helpers (temp dirs, `open_hand_list`). Only its `@(test)` registration is inside `when #config(KV_BENCH, false)`, so every normal run and every cross-target `odin check` still compiles and type-checks the body.
- **`-define:KV_STEADY_COMMITS=n`** (default 10⁴) lets the two long steady-state tests run shorter while iterating. On macOS they take about 3 minutes per configuration.
- **The isolation test's writer is paced by reader progress.** Unpaced, a fast `fsync` (or a stubbed one) let the writer finish before readers overlapped with it.

**Deliberate-bug checks.** Each mutation was applied to a fresh scratch copy of `kv/` and `tests/`, never to the repository's sources. `os_sync` was stubbed in the copy for speed, except for the TSan check, and every run was wrapped in `timeout -s KILL`.
1. *Over-eager horizon* (`return oldest + 1`):
   - `test_model_randomized` fails at its first held reader ("get found an absent key").
   - The isolation test fails in both phases: frozen readers see wrong values; churn readers see keys out of order and wrong entry counts. The churn phase was rechecked after pacing was added.
2. *Reuse disabled in `page_alloc`:* all three steady-state tests fail.
   - The plateau file reaches 138,935 pages.
   - After the long reader, puts add 12,940 pages.
   - The full-map test hits `Map_Full` at its first commit.
3. *Runs never taken from reusable pages* (single pages still are): all three fail. The plateau reaches 30,628 pages, puts add 2,898 after the long reader, and the full map hits `Map_Full` at commit 10.
4. *`env_stats`:*
   - no update after the release at begin: "the release at begin is not reported";
   - no update at commit, or no `file_pages` update in `file_grow`: several mismatches;
   - counting slots instead of readers: 2 instead of 3.
   - All fail `test_env_stats`.
5. *`env_stats` without the lock*, under TSan with real syncs: a data race between `env_stats` and `reader_register` is reported in 2 of 3 runs.
6. *The horizon ignores readers* (only `S − 1`): the held-reader model test fails for 4 of 4 seeds.
7. *`<` for `≤` in the release* (only delays reuse): no integrity check fails in the model, steady-state, `env_stats` or isolation tests. The only failure was the isolation test's read-count check with `os_sync` stubbed, which led to the pacing above.
