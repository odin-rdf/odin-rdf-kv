# Delete verification: model, readers, churn and fill measurement

## Parent Initiative

[[KV-I-0003]]

## Objective

Show that `del` holds up under randomized and concurrent use and in the steady state (KV-I-0003 NFR-003, NFR-004, D1), measure how full trees are after deletes, and close the initiative.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [x] The randomized model (`tests/model.odin`, `run_model`) includes `del` of present and absent keys against the oracle, with `space_check` after every commit and reopen, held readers checked against their snapshots, and full scans in both directions.
- [x] NFR-003: a reader holding a snapshot across a commit that deletes half the keys (some with overflow values) still reads every one of them, then the pages are reused after it ends.
- [x] The threaded isolation test includes deletes and passes under `-sanitize:thread` on macOS arm64, run more than once.
- [x] NFR-004: a churn steady state (inserts and deletes over a moving key set, no readers) keeps `last_pgno` bounded, on demand with the other steady tests (`KV_STEADY`).
- [x] Deleting every key and committing gives the empty tree, with every page but the meta pages on the free list or its run (`space_check`), and the file reused by a following reload.
- [x] Fill measurement: average and minimum leaf and branch fill after deleting 50% and 90% of keys at random, against an insert-only tree. Reported in the Results, not asserted.
- [x] `scripts/test.sh`, `scripts/test.sh --steady` once, and `scripts/test-linux.sh` on arm64 and amd64 pass.
- [x] The vision's Current State, `CLAUDE.md` (next up, code map) and the initiative's Results section (exit criteria table, deviations, known limitations) are updated.

## Implementation Notes

### Technical Approach
- Weight the model's operation mix so the tree both grows and shrinks, including phases that empty it, so root collapse and regrowth from `root = 0` are exercised.
- The churn workload should delete keys that span overflow values and different key sizes, so merges happen on leaves and branches.

### Dependencies
KV-T-0017.

### Risk Considerations
- A low fill after random deletes is the known cost of D1 (no borrowing). If it is severe, record it and file borrowing as a backlog item rather than changing the design here.

## Status Updates

### 2026-09-24: model done; readers, isolation, churn and fill next

- `run_model` has deletes and **tides**: a rising tide of 10–40 commits (40% put, 10% del), then a falling one (10% put, 40% del) until a commit leaves the tree empty, capped at 200 commits. 80% of deletes target a present key (the first present one from a random position in key order), 20% a random id, which may be absent. A delete returning `Map_Full` part-way fails the run, as for put.
- `test_model_randomized` (3 seeds): about 27,000 puts, 18,000–19,000 deletes, 4,000 absent deletes, 6–8 commits that emptied the tree, 110–116 held readers over about 930 commits, 17,000 reused pages. New expectations: dels > 10,000, absent_dels > 1,000, empty_commits ≥ 2; the put floors dropped to 20,000 (2,000 overflow).
- `test_model_until_map_full` runs with `tides = false` (a rising tide only, still 10% deletes), because with falling tides the data never outgrows the map. About 80% of keys stay present, so the map went from 5 MiB (never filled) to 4 MiB: it fills after 60–93 commits over 4 seeds.

### 2026-09-24: readers, isolation, churn, fill

- **Isolation** (`test_snapshot_isolation_across_threads`): a third of the writer's operations are deletes, in both phases (after an insert-only initial load). About 2,300 deletes land under the churning readers. Clean under `-sanitize:thread` twice, with no reports.
- **NFR-003** (`test_del_reader_keeps_snapshot`): a reader held across a commit deleting every even key of 2,000 (over 100 in overflow runs) and 20 more commits of deletes and re-inserts reads all 2,000. Nothing is reusable while it's held; after it ends, reloading the deleted half doesn't grow the file.
- **Delete everything** (`test_del_everything_then_reload`): 1,000 steady keys deleted in one commit give the empty tree; free pages plus the free list's run account for every page. Reloading the same data after one more commit adds 6 pages to 351, not 0: the reload can't reuse the pages of the snapshot it began from (a one-key tree and its two-page free-list run, D1), its own run needs a page, and overflow values need contiguous runs. It asserts ≤ 3%.
- **Churn steady state** (`steady_state_churn`, under `KV_STEADY`): a window of 1,000 ids slides forward (delete the lowest, insert the next) while random ids inside it are toggled. At 1,000 commits: loaded at 352 pages, 369 at 500 and at 1,000 commits; the second half wrote 2,774 pages and added none.
- **Fill** (`tests/fill_test.odin`, `test_bench_delete_fill`, under `KV_BENCH`), 50,000 keys of 8 bytes with 0–100-byte values, two seeds:

| Tree | Depth | Leaves | Leaf fill | Min leaf | Branches | Branch fill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| inserted, random order | 3 | 1,212–1,225 | 68–69% | 48.8% | 9 | 66–67% |
| 50% deleted at random | 3 | 1,049–1,054 | 39.5–39.7% | 25.0% | 9 | 57–58% |
| the survivors, inserted | 3 | 589–590 | 70.5–70.7% | 49% | 5 | 58% |
| 90% deleted at random | 3 | 181–193 | 43–46% | 25.2–25.3% | 3 | 30–32% |
| the survivors, inserted | 2 | 120–123 | 67.5–69.5% | 49.5% | 1 | 59–60% |

  So after random deletes a tree has about 1.6–1.8× the leaves an insert-only tree of the same keys has, and can be a level deeper. No leaf was under 25% in either run, and no branch below the root had a single child: the no-fill-guarantee case of D1 (an underfull page with two siblings too full to merge) didn't occur in this workload. A merged pair holds at most a page, so pages hover just above 25% where merges stop. Borrowing isn't worth filing on this evidence.
